Second, I prioritize strategic trade-offs that balance mission and business sustainability. At [Ecometro](/case-studies/ecometro-product-strategy), I demonstrated this by convincing the board to initially target enterprise clients—not as a departure from their sustainability mission, but as a strategic path to eventually democratize their technology. Business model and social impact aren't opposing forces, but interconnected strategies.

Lastly, I embrace continuous learning through phased releases, close partner collaboration, and a willingness to challenge existing assumptions. My work consistently shows that products evolve through direct user feedback, not theoretical perfection. ### Question
How do you work?

### Response
I approach product management and technical development with a structured, collaborative, and iterative methodology. My workflow centers on three key principles: evidence-based decision making, continuous validation, and cross-functional teamwork.

Take my work at [RatedPower](/case-studies/ratedpower-topography) as an example. I always prioritize shipping in phases, validating with real users between releases. This allows me to de-risk complex features and ensure we're building something that genuinely solves customer problems. I embed myself directly in the feedback loop, maintaining close relationships with Customer Success teams and a core group of power users who help me validate ideas before full implementation.

In my recent [portfolio website project](/case-studies/portfolio), I took this approach even further by using an advanced agent-based development process called "Ralph Wiggum". This autonomous workflow allows specialized AI agents to work through implementation plans, run tests, and iterate independently, with human review happening at key phase completions. This dramatically accelerated development while maintaining strict quality gates like test-driven development (TDD) and comprehensive code reviews.
