# Personal Biography - Mario Bennekers

## Origins & Family Background

I was born in Granada, Spain. My mother is Spanish and my father is Dutch, which explains my surname and my Nordic appearance. I grew up in Aguadulce, a small town very close to Almería known as a popular summer vacation destination.

My parents were a bit hippie-ish and met when my mother was living alone in the Albaicín (the most beautiful neighborhood in Granada) while completing her medical residency. My father is a graphic designer, which influenced me from a very young age. I was always in his office watching him design things, and as soon as I could, I was using his Mac to experiment with Photoshop and Freehand to create my own designs.

During my childhood, I traveled extensively with my parents. I always saw my father as a role model since he had traveled to many places before settling in Spain. My mother was also a woman ahead of her time.

## Education & Early Strengths

I was always especially good at mathematics, physics, and technical drawing. I even participated in a national mathematics olympiad.

I studied Civil Engineering in Granada. Given my natural aptitude for technical subjects, it was a logical path to take. Honestly, if I had the chance to choose again today, I would definitely study Computer Science instead. But I still loved my degree and I believe it gave me strong foundations in mathematics, physics, project organization, and problem-solving in general.

## International Experience & Career in Solar Energy

During university, I completed two exchange programs: I lived for a full year in Istanbul, Turkey, and half a year in Valparaíso, Chile. In Chile, I finished my civil engineering degree and decided to stay for an internship to complete my training in Santiago de Chile, working for Solarpack.

Shortly after, I was offered the opportunity to work as a Site Manager on my first solar plant in Pozo Almonte, a town near Iquique in northern Chile, right in the middle of the Atacama Desert. The experience of working in the Atacama Desert was incredible, and I also took advantage of being there to learn how to surf, since Iquique is a spectacular place for it.

My next project was in the interior of Uruguay, at the triple border between Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay. After that, I went to India as a Project Manager to manage my biggest project yet, in the province of Telangana. There, I was responsible for developing two projects totaling 40 megawatts. It was a truly incredible experience, but also exhausting and somewhat tough at times.

## The Career Pivot

At the end of 2017, after almost 3 years working as a Project Manager with great growth opportunities in that role, I decided it wasn't the path I wanted to follow. I had been away from Spain since early 2014. This job had given me the opportunity to live in very special places and travel throughout the Americas and Asia, but I decided I wanted to return to Europe and change careers to something that fit my personality better—I just didn't know exactly what that could be.

So I decided to take some time to think about what direction I wanted to take, and I did so by pursuing a project I had been dreaming about since my time at university in Chile when I did my first backpacking trip: crossing South America from Patagonia to Colombia, exploring the entire Andes mountain range and the Pacific coast.

It was an incredible experience that allowed me to dive into two of my main passions: photography and mountains. During rest periods, I started taking a programming course, which sparked my curiosity about startups and tech companies.

## Finding Product Management

When I returned to Spain, I found RatedPower, which turned out to be the perfect place for the change I wanted to make—a startup that automated the design of the solar plants I had been building for the past 3 years. It was a perfect fit for me. Shortly after starting, it became clear that my role as an expert in solar engineering, specifically in civil works, would make a difference.

I also began studying Product Management and fell in love with it. It was as if all the pieces fell into place. I liked project management but found it somewhat tedious, especially in construction. Everything that didn't quite fit for me in projects, I found in product management—things like the ability to learn from customers or run experiments. This is where my career as a Product Manager began.

## Living in the Netherlands

When COVID started, my partner had the opportunity to work in the Netherlands, and we decided to move to Rotterdam. The opportunity to live in my father's country near my Dutch family was something I had always wanted to do.

I worked remotely for RatedPower for a while, but once I was there, I had the opportunity to work at Maxem, a startup trying to revolutionize the electric mobility sector with renewable energy. I loved it—it was a sector in full growth, and the Netherlands is one of the top 5 countries in EV adoption. It was a perfect opportunity to continue expanding my knowledge in renewables and technologies that could have an impact in the fight against climate change.

## Return to Spain & Freelancing

After 2 years living in Rotterdam and Amsterdam, we decided to return to Spain to be closer to our families and moved to Madrid. Despite having the possibility to continue working remotely for Maxem, I decided I wanted to take a leap and start my own venture using the product knowledge I had accumulated over the years.

That's how I started working as a freelancer, offering my services first to Maxem, naturally, and then to other companies like Ecometro and Imageryst. I used this period to polish my programming skills, which I saw could greatly enhance my career with all this AI technology emerging.

To learn, I decided to build a personal project alongside my consulting work, together with a former RatedPower colleague with whom I had managed one of the most important teams at that company. We created a game based on the NFT concept but applied to a mobile geolocation game. This has been a super interesting project that allowed me, for the first time, to own the entire process from conceptualization to implementation and go-to-market—something I found absolutely fascinating.

## Languages

- **Spanish**: Native language
- **English**: Professional proficiency (C1 certified). I have worked in environments where English was the primary communication language, such as in India, at RatedPower, and at Maxem Energy Solutions.
- **Dutch**: Very good spoken level, slightly lower written level. It's a language I never formally studied but learned through conversations with my Dutch family, with whom I have a very close relationship.

## Hobbies & Interests

### Mountains & Outdoors
I love going to the mountains—whether it's trekking, trail running, cycling, but especially rock climbing and mountaineering.

### Photography
I'm passionate about photography. Since living in the Netherlands, I've become especially fond of analog photography, where I not only take the photos but also handle the entire development and printing process myself.

### Water Sports
I love water sports. My father is an avid sailor and passed that passion on to me. We have a sailboat in Aguadulce where I usually take trips in the summer. When I'm in Almería, I also practice kitesurfing.

### Music, Film & Art
I'm crazy about music, especially 2000s bands like The Strokes and Arctic Monkeys and all the indie scene that grew from there. I love films, especially weird thriller or horror movies—the Sitges Film Festival is my favorite. I'm also passionate about art. In fact, CatchIT! is a reflection of this passion. I don't just appreciate art from institutional museums but also art created by less mainstream illustrators who do it without it being their main economic activity—simply out of pure love for art.

### Reading & Meditation
I love reading. If I had to highlight someone whose work I've been especially hooked on, it would be Yuval Noah Harari—I've read all his books. He's also one of the main reasons I started meditating, which I've been doing daily for the past 3 years.

## Values & What Drives Me

Professionally, I'm guided by curiosity and ethics. I want my work to have a positive impact, which is why most of the companies I've worked for have been related to sustainability.

I'm an incredibly curious and caring person. If I believe a client or colleague has a problem, I can't stop until I fully understand the problem and try to find a solution if it's within my reach.

My friends usually describe me as a good person—someone easy to deal with and with a natural sense of agency, always looking for ways to improve the situations and people around them. I'm a warm and affectionate person. But I'm also a firm leader when necessary. I know how to differentiate situations well, and when boundaries need to be set, I set them without hesitation.

## Fun Facts & Special Skills

**Spatial orientation**: I have an incredible spatial ability. It's always been this way—for example, in technical drawing at university, I didn't need to study; it's like I knew how to do things without having to think about them much.

Since I was 12 years old, when traveling with my parents, I was the one organizing the route of where we would go, walking down the street with an open map guiding my family. It's something I love doing, which is also why I enjoy mountains and sailing so much—places where this skill is very useful.

**Favorite places to travel**: As long as there's some sea or mountains, I'm happy. It's not that I don't like cities—I do—but the sea and mountains are my true passion.
