## Context

- Building a location-based gaming platform from scratch
- What was the market opportunity?
- What problem were you solving?
- What was the vision?

**Answer:**

It all started as a cool thing to try the new AI tools for coding, but we saw an opportunity after the fall of the NFT market. It seemed like everyone was neglecting the idea but we thought it was actually a good technology that had been misused. It was then when we started thinking, what if we take the main mechanics of NFTs: having limited copies of a digital asset like and illustration or photography, and combine it with geolocation mechanics like pokemon go. So players would find real treasures in the form of digital illustrations that were created and hidden by other players, add them to their collection and trade their assets with other users so they could complete their collections.

The market opportunity came from considering the differentiation of creating something in between a game and a social media platform that will push the users to go out to the street and discover new art. It could be seen as a gamified social platform for discovering art while exercising. It was something never seen in the market, combining user generated content with a geolocation game mechanic.

The problem we were solving was for the users that just wanted to play a game that we were giving them the opportunity to play something similar to pokemon go but simulating the art world with the possibility of finding real treasures that only a limited amount of people could have. And for the users that wanted to create content, taking into account that without their content the app won't make sense and wont be fun at all, we want them to see the app not only as a way of gamifying their art but also as a way to show their work to new audiences and even as a new channel to monetize their work.

The vision has always been to create a platform where consuming art becomes something fun and something that user from every age can angage with in a fun way. We want to become a fun and healthy art discovery and marketplace for everyone.

## Discovery & Research

[TODO: How did you understand the problem?]

- Market analysis

**Answer:**

First we did market research. We reviewed competitor products, customer reviews, and app store rankings to understand existing solutions, common pain points, and where expectations weren’t being met. We mapped the competitive landscape, analyzed feature sets and pricing models of similar tools, and synthesized user feedback from forums, reviews, and social channels to uncover unmet needs and recurring frustrations.

We found out that the geolocation genre is not so exploded as other genres and found out that there were not so many competitors on the genre. Geolocation gaming: $1.2 billion revenue (2024). Opportunity in concentration: While top 3 lead, the genre is far from saturated, offering substantial revenue and download growth.

We also found out that the geolocation games have very good retention metrics compared to other genres. They are games where players stick and play on the for the long run which meant we had the opportunity to build a game that players will play for a long time.

https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf

https://sensortower.com/state-of-gaming-2025

We started right away interviewing, at the beggining we went mainly to art fairs of Micro illustrator influencers with around 10k followers, where we could speak with them and with their fan bases. This is the place were we found more potential users, both for collectors and for creators.

After we launched the MVP in the form of a webapp, We combined a lightweight quant study (surveys and usage data where available) with qualitative inputs (interviews and review mining) to identify who was struggling most, what workflows were breaking down, and which problems were important enough that users were already hacking together their own workarounds.

**Key decisions made:**
- Chose pixel-art aesthetic to differentiate from competitors
- Implemented freemium model with cosmetic purchases only
- Built AI chatbot for user engagement and support
- Focused on single-player experience first before social features

**Answer**

First we decided to build our MVP with what we thought was the most fundamental mechanic in the game: which was creating collection, collecting it from the map and trading. 

One main thing we decided to leave out of the early stages of development was to implement NFTs or any type of blockchain technology. What we found from our early interviews was that our potential player had a lot of aversion to this technology. After the NFT downfall marketing an app with that concept was seen as a scam, wether when we were talking about the mechanics of the game without mentioning the NFT concept we had great reception of the idea. So we decided to leave it out as it was harming more than helping and also it was less work, so it made sense not to do it.
